# Worked Answer Key

## Page 1
1. $3/8$: 3 of 8 equal parts are shaded. 2. Numerator 5 counts the parts taken; denominator 7 tells how many equal parts make the whole. 3. Any bar split into 6 equal parts with 4 shaded; $4/6$. Yes, the parts must be equal in size so that each represents $1/6$ of the whole.

## Page 2
1. $3/5$ of 20 = $20\div5\times3=12$. Draw 5 groups of 4 counters and circle 3 groups: 12 counters. 2. Blue $7/12$; not blue $5/12$. 3. $24\div4=6$ students.

## Page 3
1. $1/9$. 2. Divide a strip into 8 equal pieces. Each is $1/8$ of the strip, and all 8 pieces together make 1 whole. 3. For the same whole, $1/3=2/6>1/6$.

## Page 4
1. $7/4=1\frac34$ (4 goes into 7 once, remainder 3). 2. $2\frac35=(2\times5+3)/5=13/5$. 3. One whole plus 1 of 2 parts; $1\frac12=3/2$.

## Page 5
1. Six equal intervals from 0 to 1; the fourth tick after 0 is $4/6$. 2. $3/8$. 3. $5/4=1\frac14$; mark one whole and one fourth.

## Page 6
1. Multiply by 4: $2/3=8/12$. 2. Multiply by 3: $5/8=15/24$. 3. Examples: $6/14$ and $9/21$.

## Page 7
1. Divide by 6: $12/18=2/3$. 2. Divide by 5: $20/25=4/5$. 3. Yes: $18\div6=3$ and $24\div6=4$, so $18/24=3/4$.

## Page 8
1. Split each half of the same whole into 4 equal pieces. The whole now has 8 equal pieces, and one half covers 4 of them: $1/2=4/8$. 2. Examples: $1/2$ and $6/12$ are equivalent to $3/6$; other equivalent fractions are valid. 3. True: multiply both numerator and denominator by 2: $\frac{2\times2}{5\times2}=\frac4{10}$.

## Page 12

## Page 13
1. $2/3=8/12$, so $=$. 2. Multiply both numerator and denominator by 3: $5/6=15/18$, so $=$. 3. Neither is greater: multiplying both numerator and denominator of $3/5$ by 3 gives $9/15$, so they are equal.

## Page 14
1. Draw two equal-size rectangles. Split one into 3 equal parts with 2 shaded; split the other into 9 equal parts with 6 shaded. Both show the same amount: $2/3=6/9$. 2. Draw a rectangle divided into 4 equal parts and shade 3. Split each fourth into 3 equal smaller parts. Label the shaded amount $3/4$ and $9/12$: the same area is shaded. 3. Draw 15 objects in 5 groups of 3. Shade all the objects in 2 groups: 2 of 5 groups and 6 of 15 objects are shaded, so $2/5=6/15$.

## Page 15
1. Split every part of the same whole into 3 equal smaller parts. There are now 3 times as many parts in the whole and 3 times as many selected parts, but the selected amount is unchanged. 2. The denominator also changes; $4/8$ and $1/2$ are equal, not greater. 3. Multiply both numerator and denominator by 4: $5/6=20/24$.

## Page 16
1. $9/4=2\frac14$; converting back gives $(2\times4+1)/4=9/4$. 2. $1\frac23=(1\times3+2)/3=5/3$. 3. Yes: $2\frac12=(2\times2+1)/2=5/2$.

## Page 17
1. If 4 counters are $2/3$, one third is 2 counters, so the whole is $2\times3=6$ counters. 2. $6\div3=2$ per fifth; $2\times5=10$ tiles. 3. $15\div5=3$ per eighth; $3\times8=24$ cm.

## Page 18

## Page 19
1. $24/32$ divides by 8 to $3/4$; denominator 20 requires multiply by 5: $15/20$. 2. $18/30$ divides by 6 to $3/5$; denominator 5 fraction is $3/5$. 3. $3/4<4/4<3/2$ (0.75, 1, 1.5).

## Page 20
1. $18/24$ divides by 6 to $3/4$; multiply by 2 for denominator 8: $6/8$. 2. Examples: $8/10$ (×2), $12/15$ (×3), $20/25$ (×5); each multiplier applies to both numerator and denominator. Other sets of three different equivalent fractions are valid. 3. Multiply numerator and denominator by the same nonzero whole number; $\frac{7\times4}{10\times4}=\frac{28}{40}$, so the missing numerator is 28.
