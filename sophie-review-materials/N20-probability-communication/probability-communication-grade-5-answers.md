# Worked Answer Key

**Page 1.** 1. Certain: a die always gives one of 1–6. 2. Blue; $9/10$ is greater than $1/10$.

**Page 2.** 1. {heads, tails}. 2. {1, 2, 3, 4}.

**Page 3.** 1. One favorable outcome out of six: $1/6$. 2. Even outcomes are 2, 4, 6: $3/6=1/2$.

**Page 4.** 1. Total $4+3+1=8$; P(yellow) $=3/8$. 2. Not purple: $7/8$.

**Page 5.** 1. $0$; blue is impossible because none is in the bag. 2. $12/12=1$; certain.

**Page 6.** 1. Not orange: $5/8$. 2. $1-1/5=4/5$.

**Page 7.** 1. A: $2/8=1/4$; B: $4/12=1/3$; Bag B is greater. 2. $3/10=0.30$, $1/4=0.25$; $3/10$ is greater.

**Page 8.** 1. Yes. Heads and tails each have probability $1/2$, so each player has an equal chance. 2. No. Red is $3/4$ and blue is $1/4$, so chances are unequal.

**Page 9.** 1. P(6) $=1/6$; $60\div6=10$, so about 10 sixes. 2. Green is $5/10=1/2$; $40\div2=20$.

**Page 10.** 1. $17/30$. 2. $9/25$.

**Page 11.** 1. $18/120=3/20$; $600\times3/20=90$. 2. $12/20=3/5$; $100\times3/5=60$.

**Page 12.** 1. Total $6+5+3+2=16$; P(green) $=3/16$. 2. P(red or blue) $=(6+5)/16=11/16$.

**Page 13.** 1. {HH, HT, TH, TT}; one favorable outcome, so $1/4$. 2. HT and TH are favorable: $2/4=1/2$.

**Page 14.** 1. Color pairs are RR, RB, BR, BB; P(RR) $=(2/3)(2/3)=4/9$. 2. P(RB or BR) $=(2/3)(1/3)+(1/3)(2/3)=4/9$.

**Page 15.** 1. No. Probability must be from 0 to 1; $7/4>1$. 2. The claim is false. $1/2$ describes long-run expectation; a small number of trials can differ.

**Page 16.** 1. Total $12$; P(apple) $=5/12$ (5 favorable apples over 12 fruits). 2. 0 means no favorable outcomes, such as rolling a 7 on a six-sided die. 1 means every outcome is favorable, such as rolling a number from 1–6 on that die.

**Page 17.** 1. Books or games: $3+2=5$ favorable spaces out of 10, so $1/2$. 2. Other fruit: $24-15=9$; $9/24=3/8$.

**Page 18.** 1. Use 3 equal sections labeled for the three players; each has $1/3$, so it is fair. 2. Use 4 equal sections with 1 blue and 3 other colors; blue is $1/4$.

**Page 19.** 1. Total $12$; not green is $3+4=7$, so $7/12$. 2. Odd outcomes (1, 3, 5) have probability $3/6=1/2$; $90\times1/2=45$, assuming a fair die and similar independent rolls.

**Page 20.** 1. Greater than 4: {5, 6}, so $2/6=1/3$. Red tile: $3/10$. Since $1/3>3/10$, rolling greater than 4 is more likely. 2. Theoretical probability = favorable equally likely outcomes ÷ total equally likely outcomes. Less than 3 means 1 or 2: $2/6=1/3$.
