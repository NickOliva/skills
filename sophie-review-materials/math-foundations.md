# Elementary Math Foundations

A compact guide to understanding numbers, calculating, measuring, and recognizing patterns.

## Need to know

- **Numbers and place value:** Whole numbers are 0, 1, 2, and so on. A digit's position determines its value: 352 means 3 hundreds, 5 tens, and 2 ones. Each place to the left is worth ten times as much. On a number line, numbers increase toward the right. Use `>` for greater than, `<` for less than, and `=` for equal to.
- **Four operations:** Addition combines amounts; subtraction finds a difference or what remains. Multiplication counts equal groups. Division splits an amount into equal groups or finds how many groups fit. Addition and subtraction undo each other, as do multiplication and division when dividing by a nonzero number.
- **Fractions and decimals:** A fraction describes equal parts. In `3/4`, the denominator, 4, tells how many equal parts make one whole; the numerator, 3, counts those parts. Equivalent fractions name the same amount: `1/2 = 2/4`. Decimals extend place value to tenths, hundredths, and beyond: `0.25 = 25/100 = 1/4`.
- **Shapes and measurement:** A triangle has three straight sides. A rectangle has four right angles; a square is a rectangle with four equal sides. Perimeter measures distance around a shape. Area measures the surface it covers. Use length units, such as centimeters, for perimeter and square units for area. Compare measurements using the same units; for time, 60 minutes equals 1 hour.
- **Patterns and data:** A pattern follows a rule, such as adding 3: 3, 6, 9, 12. Tables and graphs organize information. Read their labels and scales before comparing values. An equation states that two expressions have the same value; `5 + 2 = 7` is one example.

## Examples

- **Add using place value:** `27 + 15 = 27 + 10 + 5 = 42`. Check by subtracting: `42 − 15 = 27`.
- **Connect multiplication and division:** Four bags holding 3 apples each contain `4 × 3 = 12` apples. Sharing those 12 apples equally among 4 people gives `12 ÷ 4 = 3` each.
- **Add like fractions:** `1/4 + 2/4 = 3/4`. Count the quarter-sized pieces; their size stays the same.
- **Measure a rectangle:** A rectangle 5 cm long and 3 cm wide has perimeter `5 + 3 + 5 + 3 = 16 cm` and area `5 × 3 = 15 cm²`.

## Common mix-ups

- `=` means both sides have equal value, not simply “write the answer next.”
- More decimal digits do not guarantee a larger number: `0.5 = 0.50 > 0.45`.
- Multiplication and division come before addition and subtraction. Handle parentheses first; work left to right among operations of equal priority.
- You cannot divide by zero. But `0 ÷ 4 = 0`.
