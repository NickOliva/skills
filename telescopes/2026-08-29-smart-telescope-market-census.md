# US Portable Smart-Telescope Market Census

**Research date:** 2026-08-29  
**Scope:** New equipment available to a US buyer, with a complete observing kit plausibly at or below $2,000 before tax. Used equipment is outside scope. Prices and stock can change quickly.

## What the categories mean

The convenient products split into three optical groups:

- **30–35 mm, 150–160 mm wide-field cameras** (Seestar S30 Pro and the two DWARFs) are genuinely bag-sized. They frame large nebulae and Andromeda well, especially with a mosaic, but a small galaxy occupies few pixels. Their short focal lengths also render Jupiter and Saturn as very small disks.
- **50–53 mm, 200–260 mm systems** (Vespera II and the Spectrum/MirroSky products) collect more light or put more pixels across a small object. They are still wide-field deep-sky instruments, not planetary telescopes. Andromeda is about **3° across**—roughly six full Moons—so a field narrower than that needs a mosaic to show the faint outer disk, not merely its bright core. [NASA gives the same 3° scale](https://eclipse2017.nasa.gov/exploring-angular-diameter).
- **Long-focal-length planetary optics** want much more aperture, focal length, and high-frame-rate video. The Spectrum SP127 includes a 127 mm Maksutov, but its documented smart-imaging path is the piggyback 53 mm camera scope; that distinction matters. Live-stacking many short deep-sky exposures does not substitute for planetary sampling.

The advertised “equivalent focal length” on a phone-style camera describes crop/field of view, not extra optical resolving power. The aperture, real focal length, sensor, and pixel scale are the useful numbers.

## Current eligible products

### ZWO Seestar S30 Pro

**Status, cost, and portability.** ZWO lists it in stock at $699. The price includes the telescope, tabletop tripod, soft case, USB-C cable, and magnetic solar filter; the main unit is 1.65 kg/3.64 lb and 210 × 140 × 80 mm. ZWO does not publish a packed-kit weight, but the complete small case is comfortably below the 30 lb limit. The manufacturer describes a three-step setup, and its own hands-on material says the system is ready in minutes. [Product specifications and stock](https://us.seestar.com/products/seestar-s30-pro); [first-party package description](https://us.seestar.com/blogs/review/a-veteran-s-review-of-the-zwo-seestar-s30-pro-smart-telescope).

The included tabletop tripod is sufficient for normal Alt-Az use. Equatorial mode needs a tilting head: ZWO sells the TH10 from $99 and the TC20 carbon tripod for $99; its own FAQ calls the TH10 essential for EQ and recommends the TH10 + TC20 combination. Thus the relevant totals are $699 Alt-Az, $798 minimum EQ, or $897 with ZWO’s recommended EQ support. [Accessory prices](https://us.zwoastro.com/collections/seestar); [EQ requirement](https://us.seestar.com/products/seestar-s30-pro).

**Optics and imaging.** The telephoto path is a 30 mm, 160 mm f/5.3 four-element ED apochromat with an IMX585, 3840 × 2160/8.3 MP sensor. The stated 4.6° telephoto field is the diagonal; from the published sensor and focal length, the rectangular field is about 4.0° × 2.25°. A second IMX586 camera provides a 63° wide view. Built-in UV/IR-cut, dark, and O III/H-alpha dual-band filters, plus active anti-dew heating, are standard. It saves JPEG and FITS, video as MP4/AVI, and has 128 GB eMMC storage. [Full manufacturer specification](https://us.seestar.com/products/seestar-s30-pro).

The integrated mount is Alt-Az with an EQ mode. Live stacking, autofocus, plate solving/GoTo, a multi-target Plan mode, and post-processing are app functions. Up to 60-second sub-exposures are supported only in EQ mode; the manufacturer does not state the Alt-Az maximum on the current specification page. No subscription is required for local operation or image capture.

**iPad, offline use, and power.** Apple lists Seestar as a native iPad app requiring iPadOS 13 or later, not merely an enlarged iPhone app. [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/seestar/id6447429891). After initial activation and firmware/app installation, S30 Pro connects directly to its own Wi-Fi hotspot and works without internet or cellular service. [ZWO offline FAQ](https://us.seestar.com/blogs/faq/seestar-s30-pro-faq). The 6,000 mAh battery is rated for about six hours and supports USB-C power while observing.

**Target fit.** Its roughly 4° horizontal field can contain Andromeda’s approximately 3° outer disk in one correctly rotated telephoto frame; it also supports DSO framing and wide-field panorama features. The 30 mm aperture and 160 mm focal length favor large galaxies, nebulae, and clusters over small galaxies. For planets, ZWO itself says Jupiter and Saturn are visible and basic images may show Jupiter’s moons or Saturn’s rings, but fine surface detail is limited; at this focal length they span only a small number of native pixels. [ZWO’s planetary qualification](https://us.seestar.com/products/seestar-s30-pro).

**Evidence gaps for Agent 2:** independent short-session DSO output; real Plan-mode target-change overhead; Alt-Az sub-exposure choices; packed weight; whether the wide field creates a meaningful small-galaxy penalty versus the larger-aperture choices.

### DWARFLAB DWARF mini

**Status, cost, and portability.** B&H has the telescope in stock for $399; DWARFLAB sells it direct for $419. The telescope box contains the unit, solar ND filter, USB-C cable, and cloth but no tripod. DWARFLAB’s hydraulic mini tripod is $89, making the real total $488 using B&H for the telescope or $508 direct. A $39 padded bag is optional. The unit is 840 g/1.85 lb and 100.38 × 183.61 × 60.70 mm; the manufacturer claims three minutes from unboxing to shooting. [Product, accessories, package, and setup claim](https://www.dwarflab.com/us/products/dwarf-mini-smart-telescope); [B&H price and stock](https://www.bhphotovideo.com/c/product/1936045-REG/dwarflab_dwarf_mini_smart_telescope.html/specs).

**Optics and imaging.** The tele camera combines a 30 mm, 150 mm (approximately f/5) six-element lens with Sony IMX662, 1920 × 1080/2.1 MP, 2.9 µm pixels; the second camera is an OmniVision OS02K10 at 1920 × 1080. The calculated tele field is about 2.13° × 1.20°. It has Astro, dark, and dual-band filters and 64 GB eMMC. DWARFLAB states four-hour battery life from a 7,000 mAh battery and allows USB charging during use. [Manufacturer comparison specification](https://www.dwarflab.com/us/products/dwarf-mini-smart-telescope).

DWARF mini supports GoTo, tracking, autofocus, live stacking, scheduled shooting, calibration frames, multi-night stacking, and a four-panel astro mosaic up to 1.8 × 1.8 times the native field. The product page still says 90-second EQ exposures, while the current App Store changelog says version 3.3.8 raised the mini to **180 seconds**; this firmware/app discrepancy should be verified on the shipping unit. Mosaic imaging also requires EQ orientation. [Current user manual and 1.8 × 1.8 mosaic limit](https://help.dwarflab.com/en/docs/DWARF-mini-Smart-Telescope-User-Manual); [App Store version history](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/dwarflab/id1667394131?platform=ipad).

The app exports raw data for external processing; first-party material advertises frame selection, dark/bias/flat calibration, and multi-night stacking, while the manual confirms 64 GB storage. FITS should be verified at the file level because the mini page says “RAW” rather than naming every exported format. [Raw/export and calibration description](https://www.dwarflab.com/us/products/dwarf-mini-smart-telescope); [storage manual](https://help.dwarflab.com/en/docs/DWARF-mini-Smart-Telescope-User-Manual).

**iPad, offline use, and power.** The DWARFLAB app is listed for iPad and iPhone and requires iPadOS 15 or later. Version 3.4.0 added seamless multi-target schedules. [App Store listing and history](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/dwarflab/id1667394131?platform=ipad). In AP mode, the iPad connects directly to the telescope and does not need internet. Internet is needed for initial activation, updates, online atlas downloads, log upload, and cloud functions; preloading the atlas and activating at home is prudent. [DWARFLAB AP-mode documentation](https://help.dwarflab.com/en/docs/DWARF-3-Smart-Telescope-User-Manual-Part1-App-Interface-Introduction?product=dwarf-3).

**Target fit.** Andromeda does not fit in the 2.13° native width, but a 1.8 × mosaic expands the calculated footprint to roughly 3.8° × 2.2°, enough for the galaxy. That mosaic adds capture and transition time. Resolution is only 2 MP and aperture 30 mm, so small galaxies will be the most demanding class. The Moon and solar work are supported; Jupiter and Saturn will be tiny, with little credible detail beyond moons and perhaps the gross ring shape under good conditions.

**Evidence gaps for Agent 2:** exact FITS/raw payload and bit depth; 180-second firmware behavior; mosaic duration and rejection rate; tablet stability; independent 5–30 minute examples of M31 and small galaxies.

### DWARFLAB DWARF 3

**Status, cost, and portability.** The current US price is $549. The telescope includes a bag, magnetic solar filter, USB-C cable, and cloth; the $89 tripod makes a complete $638 package. Body weight is 1.35 kg/2.98 lb and dimensions are 222 × 142 × 65 mm. [Current US product and package](https://www.dwarflab.com/us/products/dwarf-3-smart-telescope).

**Optics and imaging.** The 35 mm, 150 mm f/4.3 six-element apochromatic tele lens uses an IMX678 at 3840 × 2160/8.3 MP with 2 µm pixels; its calculated field is about 2.93° × 1.65°, consistent with DWARFLAB’s rounded “3°” claim. A separate IMX307 wide camera records 1920 × 1080. It includes VIS, Astro, and O III/H-alpha dual-band filters, 128 GB storage, IP54 protection, and a redesigned internal heat path for dew mitigation. The 10,000 mAh battery is now claimed to reach 5.5 hours of astronomical imaging at room temperature, and USB power can extend a session. [Manufacturer optics, filters, field, battery, and IP54 specification](https://www.dwarflab.com/us/products/dwarf-3-smart-telescope); [technical focal-length and frame-rate details](https://help.dwarflab.com/en/docs/DWARF-3-Unboxing-and-Quick-Setup).

The mount supports Alt-Az and EQ orientation, autofocus, GoTo, tracking, live stacking, schedule mode, multi-night stacking, and a four-panel 2 × 2 mosaic. Telephoto sub-exposures reach 60 seconds in EQ (wide camera 90 seconds). It exports JPG, PNG, TIFF, and FITS; the file system retains calibrated single frames and stacked output suitable for desktop work. [First-party format and mosaic specification](https://www.dwarflab.com/us/products/dwarf-3-smart-telescope); [file documentation](https://help.dwarflab.com/en/docs/How-to-View-and-Obtain-the-Files-on-DWARF-3?product=dwarf-3).

**iPad, offline use, and subscription.** It uses the same native iPad app and direct AP Wi-Fi mode described for DWARF mini. Initial activation, downloads, updates, support uploads, and cloud processing require internet, but pointing, capture, live stacking, and local file access do not. Current core control has no required subscription. [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/dwarflab/id1667394131?platform=ipad); [offline AP mode](https://help.dwarflab.com/en/docs/DWARF-3-Smart-Telescope-User-Manual-Part1-App-Interface-Introduction?product=dwarf-3).

**Target fit.** Andromeda’s bright disk nearly fills the native long dimension; the faint approximately 3° extent needs precise rotation and is safer in the 2 × 2 mosaic. Large nebulae and open clusters suit the field. Compared with mini, the larger sensor and 8.3 MP output retain much more detail for smaller galaxies, though 35 mm aperture still limits photons and resolving power. The tele camera records 4K/30 and 1080p/60 video, but at 150 mm focal length Jupiter and Saturn remain tiny; high frame rate cannot create missing optical scale.

**Evidence gaps for Agent 2:** whether “internal heat” prevents dew in sustained humid dark-site use; setup time for reliable EQ; short-session small-galaxy performance; native versus mosaic M31 time; current app stability and local-versus-cloud processing boundaries.

### Vaonis Vespera II

**Status, cost, and portability.** Vaonis’s direct site currently shows the pack out of stock, but authorized US retailer B&H has the body in stock at $1,590 and the required standard-tripod kit in stock for $1,640. The body is 5 kg/11 lb and 48 × 20 × 9 cm. Vaonis claims automatic initialization in under five minutes. [B&H body stock](https://www.bhphotovideo.com/c/product/1800609-REG/vaonis_ve50_ii_vespera_ii_observation_station.html/overview); [B&H tripod-kit stock and price](https://www.bhphotovideo.com/c/buy/digital-telescopes/ci/52044); [Vaonis setup and body specification](https://vaonis.com/products/vespera-2).

The telescope has an integral demister, but the optional $119 hygrometer is what enables automatic dew-heater control; the tripod plus hygrometer is $1,759. B&H’s tripod-and-backpack kit is $1,910; adding the hygrometer to that package would exceed the cap, so the backpack cannot be treated as required if automatic dew control is promised. A USB-C PD cable and charger/power bank are user-supplied. [Vaonis pack and hygrometer description](https://vaonis.com/products/vespera-ii-pack-1); [B&H power requirement](https://www.bhphotovideo.com/c/product/1800609-REG/vaonis_ve50_ii_vespera_ii_observation_station.html/overview).

**Optics and imaging.** Vespera II is a 50 mm, 250 mm f/5 quadruplet apochromat with a Sony IMX585, 3840 × 2160/8.3 MP, 2.9 µm sensor. Native field is 2.5° × 1.4°; CovalENS live mosaic expands this to 4.33° × 2.43° and up to 24 MP. It has live autofocus, Alt-Az tracking, plate solving, GoTo, live stacking, Plan My Night multi-target scheduling, and Expert Mode. No equatorial mode is offered. Vaonis says normal DSO unit exposures are 10 seconds and software derotates the stack. [Optics, field, mosaic, autofocus, and scheduling](https://vaonis.com/products/vespera-2); [10-second unit exposures and field-rotation handling](https://support.vaonis.com/portal/en/kb/articles/how-vespera).

The 25 GB internal storage can save 8-bit JPEG, 16-bit FITS subframes, and 16-bit pre-stacked TIFF. Expert Mode changes exposure and gain and acquires darks; FTP retrieves local files without a cloud pipeline. Broadband, dual-band, and solar filters are optional rather than built in. At a Bortle 2–3 site no light-pollution filter is essential for galaxies, but emission-nebula performance with and without the costly dual-band filter needs comparative testing. [File formats](https://vaonis.com/products/vespera-2); [expert controls](https://support.vaonis.com/portal/en/kb/articles/is-it-possible-to-change-vespera-passengers-camera-parameters-exposure-time-iso-etc); [local files/FTP](https://support.vaonis.com/portal/en/kb/articles/how-to-locate-your-observation-images-in-the-telescope-s-saved-files-31-7-2024).

**iPad, offline use, power, and subscription.** Singularity is a true iPad app requiring iPadOS 14 or later. [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/singularity-by-vaonis/id1515280835?platform=ipad). The telescope creates its own Wi-Fi network and does not need internet for observing after app/account preparation; internet is needed to download/update content. [Vaonis offline support](https://support.vaonis.com/portal/en/kb/articles/is-it-necessary-to-be-connected-to-the-internet-to-use-vespera). Battery life is four hours, and it can run from USB-C PD. No subscription is required for core control, stacking, local files, mosaic, or Plan My Night.

**Target fit.** Native width does not include Andromeda’s full faint disk, but CovalENS explicitly supports a full M31 mosaic. The 50 mm aperture and 250 mm focal length provide more photon collection and optical scale than the pocket 30–35 mm group, although the Alt-Az 10-second architecture remains oriented to live-stacked deep sky. Vaonis’s product page claims planet observing, but its own current user feedback on that page notes a one-second manual exposure floor made planets poor; the company also says automatic planetary video is not offered. Jupiter and Saturn should therefore be treated as tiny documentation images, not a reason to buy this model. [Vaonis Vespera II page, including planet-related limitation](https://vaonis.com/products/vespera-2).

**Evidence gaps for Agent 2:** whether the hygrometer is practically essential; 4-hour battery under cold conditions; actual CovalENS time/rejection rate for M31; target-change overhead in Plan My Night; current planetary mode behavior after 2026 updates; app/cloud longevity.

### Multi-target-evening plausibility

All four current eligible systems have automated GoTo/tracking and live stacking; Seestar, both DWARFs, and Vespera II now also document scheduled multi-target operation. At the census level, five targets at 10 minutes each consume 50 minutes of integration and ten consume 100 minutes, before initial setup, slews, autofocus, plate solving, rejected frames, and mosaics. With manufacturer setup claims of roughly 3–5 minutes, **5–10 targets in a two-to-three-hour evening is plausible on paper**, provided most are bright objects and not every target is a four-panel mosaic. It is not yet demonstrated that ten faint/small galaxies would be satisfying. Agent 2 must measure transition overhead and the 5/10/20/30-minute quality curve rather than convert feature support into a verdict.

## Conditional boundary cases

These products remain in the census because they are real, US-stocked, iPad-controlled smart-imaging packages under budget. They should not enter a final ranking until Agent 2 resolves the stated gating questions.

### Spectrum/MirroSky SPi53

The SPi53 is $599 and in stock direct. It includes the GoTo mount, tabletop tripod, carrying case, 53 mm ED helical-focus refractor, IMX662 smart camera, cables, and AC adapter. The assembled system is 4.4 lb; it needs a user-supplied 12 V/2 A field battery, for an estimated $639–679 total. The refractor/camera specification is 200 mm f/4, approximately 1.6° × 0.9°, 1920 × 1080/2.1 MP, 2.9 µm, 12-bit, and 64 GB (the latter details are specified for the same MirroSky camera in Spectrum’s manuals). [Current product and package](https://spectrumoi.com/product/spi53/); [SPi53 manual](https://spectrumoi.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/SPi-Ver.-1.4.pdf).

MirroSky supplies automatic plate solving, GoTo, tracking, dark subtraction, and live stacking; focus is manual with a Bahtinov mask. The app saves a finished image to the tablet, while “raw image files” require the Windows-only MirroSky Desktop Client; Spectrum does not publish the raw container, bit depth, or Mac path. [Raw-file documentation](https://spectrumoi.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/SPi-Ver.-1.4.pdf). Apple lists MirroSky as designed for iPad (iPadOS 12+) but it has only four ratings and a current 3.0 score. [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/mirrosky/id6474467188). The manual explicitly tells the user to allow connection “without internet,” so normal capture should work at a cellular-free site.

No subscription is documented for local control, live stacking, or the desktop client.

There is no documented DSO mosaic or multi-target schedule. Andromeda therefore does **not** fit the 1.6° frame, and 2 MP constrains small-galaxy detail. Planetary scale remains small. The low price and compact weight are real, but manual focus, external 12 V power, thin app evidence, undocumented sub-exposure limits, Windows-only raw extraction, and no documented mosaic/schedule keep it conditional.

### Spectrum/MirroSky SP127

The SP127 is $999.99 and in stock. It includes a full steel tripod, mount, 127 mm f/15 1,900 mm Maksutov, 25 mm eyepiece/diagonal, and a piggyback 53 mm f/4, 200 mm smart-imaging refractor using the same IMX662 camera. Complete assembled weight is 26 lb before batteries—inside the limit, but close enough that field power and case/cables matter. It runs from 12 V/2 A or eight AA cells. [Complete package and specification](https://spectrumoi.com/product_SP127.php).

The documented imaging system is the 53 mm scope: 1.6° × 0.9°, 1920 × 1080, 64 GB, Alt-Az tracking, live stack, manual focus, and Windows-client raw export. The large Maksutov is documented primarily for eyepiece use; Spectrum does not document a complete high-frame-rate camera attachment and stacking workflow through the 1,900 mm tube. It is therefore not valid to credit the smart-camera output with 127 mm planetary performance without testing. The manual supports direct Wi-Fi without internet and iPad uses the same MirroSky app. [SP127 setup/offline/manual focus](https://spectrumopticalinstruments.com/manuals/SP127_v14.pdf); [raw export](https://spectrumoi.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/SP127-Ver.-1.4.pdf).

No subscription is documented.

It lacks documented mosaic and schedule functions, cannot frame full M31 through the smart imager, sits at 26 lb, and looks much more like the conventional tripod telescope Nick is trying to avoid. It remains a boundary case only because it technically clears price, weight, iPad, and offline requirements on paper and might supply a planetary complement if the undocumented camera path works.

### Spectrum/MirroSky GX35 and HX35

These are modular smart-mount/imager packages rather than sealed smart telescopes. GX35 is $999 and HX35 $1,399, both in stock. Each includes a 53 mm f/4, 200 mm ED guide/imaging scope, IMX662 camera, mount, cables, AC adapter, and case. GX adds a 4 lb counterweight; HX uses a strain-wave RA drive and has a 9 lb mount head. Neither includes the required full-size 3/8-inch tripod or a 12 V/3 A field battery, giving estimated practical totals of $1,149–1,249 and $1,549–1,649. [GX35 package](https://spectrumoi.com/home.php/product/gx35/); [HX35 package](https://spectrumoi.com/product/hx35/); [manual requirement for full tripod](https://spectrumoi.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/HX35-GX35_Manual_v1.3.pdf).

The included camera system is again 1.6° × 0.9°, 1920 × 1080, 64 GB, 12-bit. Both mounts can operate Alt-Az or EQ; live stacking and Windows-only raw download are documented. [Full mount/camera specification and raw workflow](https://spectrumopticalinstruments.com/manuals/HX35-GX35_v13.pdf). They use the iPad-native MirroSky app and direct local Wi-Fi.

No subscription is documented.

Their expensive mount capacity is largely unused by the small included imager, while adding a larger imaging OTA/camera/focuser would break the near-turnkey scope and likely the $2,000 ceiling. Full M31 still needs a mosaic that MirroSky does not document. The manuals require tripod assembly, mount-mode configuration, leveling, OTA attachment/balancing, manual focus, and—if EQ is used—polar alignment. Complete packed weight and a demonstrated sub-15-minute first-image sequence are unpublished. These are appropriate only as conditional investigations, not presumed equivalents to the four integrated products.
